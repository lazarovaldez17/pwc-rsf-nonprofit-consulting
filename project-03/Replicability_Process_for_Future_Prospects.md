## Before You Search: Internalize the 7 Shortlisting Criteria

Before running any searches, document your interpretation of all 7 criteria. This step prevents post-hoc rationalization — if you define what "strong" means before you find a candidate, your ratings are defensible. If you define it after, they are not.

The 7 criteria for RSF (from SP3 brief):

| # | Criterion | RSF-Specific Definition | Hard Filter? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Geography | HQ in Bay Area / Silicon Valley, or explicit California / Bay Area grantmaking. Partners must be reachable for an in-person meeting. | Yes — fail geography = exclude |
| 2 | Corporate Giving Programs | Runs a structured employee giving or CSR program, ideally via Benevity (RSF is enrolled) | No — but strong signal when present |
| 3 | Mission Alignment | Documents grants or programs in: youth sports, women's athletics, college access for underserved students, or youth development broadly | Yes — fail mission = exclude |
| 4 | Women's Flag Football | Active interest in women's sports, gender equity in athletics, or specifically flag football / NFL women's initiatives | No — priority booster when present |
| 5 | Workforce & Trades | Invests in workforce development or economic mobility for youth | No — deprioritize unless alignment is compelling |
| 6 | Partnership Size Fit | Corporate: $10K–$75K. Foundation: $25K–$150K. Outside range = poor fit for RSF's current stage. | Yes — fail size = exclude |
| 7 | Warm Connection Potential | Direct personal relationship, Benevity enrollment, RSF alumni network, or second-degree plausible path | No — but Strong here can elevate any lead |

**Scoring rubric (applied in the Long List tab):**
- **Strong** = meets the definition clearly with documented evidence
- **Partial** = meets part of the definition, or evidence is incomplete
- **Weak** = does not meet the definition
- **Unknown** = insufficient information; do 10 minutes of 990 research before finalizing

## The 5 Search Strategies

### Strategy 1: The Benevity Ecosystem

**Why this matters for RSF:**  
RSF is already enrolled as a vetted nonprofit on Benevity. This means any Bay Area company using Benevity becomes an immediately actionable warm lead — RSF doesn't need to be enrolled, the infrastructure already exists.

**Search terms used in this round:**
```
"Benevity" "Bay Area" OR "Silicon Valley" corporate giving employees
[company name] "youth" OR "education" OR "community investment" OR "women's sports" philanthropy
Apple corporate giving Benevity employee matching grants youth education California
Google Benevity employee matching grants youth education California
Adobe Foundation community grants youth education 2024 2025 giving range
```

**Tools used:** Web search (Google), company CSR pages (adobe.com, apple.com, google.com/community), Double the Donation database (doublethedonation.com)

**Judgment calls:**
- Apple and Google were confirmed Benevity partners through existing RSF relationships. Both were included in the long list as "Corporate Giving" candidates rather than Foundation candidates — the play here is increasing employee giving volume, not a traditional grant application.
- Adobe was elevated to a Foundation candidate because of the confirmed Zenith Prep → Adobe HQ Innovator Program warm connection, which creates a direct path to the Employee Community Fund (which is employee-nomination-driven, not a cold application).
- Driscoll's was included as a "Deepening" candidate — RSF already has this relationship via Benevity. The question is whether to propose a direct multi-year corporate partnership separate from matching. This is a relationship management task, not a new lead.
- Cisco was deprioritized: the company's community grants program appears to have sunsetted or restructured. Mission alignment was also weak (pre-K–5 education focus vs. RSF's high school/college age group).
- Salesforce was included but deprioritized: no explicit sports or college access angle, and the most relevant Salesforce unrestricted grants target Black-led nonprofits specifically, which RSF may not be positioned for.

**What to search next time:**  
```
"Benevity" "nonprofit" "youth sports" OR "college access" Bay Area 2026
[new company name] Benevity nonprofit eligible giving program
```

### Strategy 2: Candid / Foundation Directory

**Why this matters for RSF:**  
Candid (formerly GuideStar + Foundation Center) is the authoritative database for private and community foundations. It allows filtering by geography, cause area, and grantee size.

**Search terms used in this round:**
```
Silicon Valley Community Foundation youth development grants 2024 2025 application
Community Foundation Santa Cruz County grants youth programs 2024 2025 apply
Joseph Vera Long Foundation grants San Jose youth education 2024 2025
Stupski Foundation education equity grants California nonprofits 2024 2025
California Wellness Foundation grants youth 2024 2025
```

**Tools used:** Web search, Candid Foundation Directory (foundationdirectory.candid.org), Instrumentl (instrumentl.com), Inside Philanthropy (insidephilanthropy.com), Hinchilla (hinchilla.com), Cause IQ (causeiq.com)

**Judgment calls:**
- Community Foundation for Santa Cruz County (CFSCC) was elevated to Tier 1 even though it's not a cold lead — RSF already has an active co-sponsorship relationship through the JEDI Scholarship. The opportunity is to convert a restricted co-sponsorship into expanded multi-year unrestricted support. This is a relationship-deepening play, not an application.
- Silicon Valley Community Foundation (SVCF) was included but flagged with a critical geography risk: SVCF serves San Mateo and Santa Clara counties. RSF is headquartered in Santa Cruz County. Before applying, RSF must verify whether their programs extend into those counties or whether they could partner with a local organization. This is a prerequisite verification step, not an obstacle.
- Stupski Foundation was researched and immediately removed: they stopped accepting new grantee applications and are in spend-down mode (ending grants after 2027). Searching a foundation's current grantmaking status before pursuing them is non-negotiable due diligence.
- Joseph & Vera Long Foundation was included in the Final 5 as the only lead with a fully open, self-directed application process — no warm path required, no invitation needed. This makes it uniquely accessible for a first-time applicant.
- California Wellness Foundation was deprioritized: health/wellness framing is a stretch for RSF's mission without dedicated wellness programming. Not worth pursuing unless RSF develops this angle.

**What to search next time:**
```
[foundation name] 990 ProPublica [foundation name]
[foundation name] "recent grants" OR "past grantees" youth California
Candid Foundation Directory California youth sports grants under $150K
```

**Note on 990 research:** When Partnership Size Fit is "Unknown," pull the organization's 990 from ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer (projects.propublica.org/nonprofits). Look at Schedule I — it lists all grants made, to whom, and how much. This is the most reliable way to calibrate whether an org gives at RSF's scale.

### Strategy 3: LinkedIn Corporate CSR Search (Manual)

**Why this matters for RSF:**  
Most Bay Area tech companies have someone whose entire job is managing community investment or employee giving. Their LinkedIn title tells you the company has a structured giving program. Their profile often tells you what they prioritize.

**How to run this manually (guide for Lazaro or RSF):**

1. Go to LinkedIn.com and use the People search filter.
2. Set **Location**: San Francisco Bay Area / San Jose
3. Search these job titles **one at a time**:
   - `"Community Investment"` — surfaces structured giving roles at large tech companies
   - `"Corporate Social Responsibility"` — broader; includes smaller companies
   - `"Corporate Giving"` — companies with a foundation arm or matching program
   - `"Director of Philanthropy"` — companies with a formal foundation
   - `"Community Relations"` — consumer brands, sports teams, regional employers
4. For each company you find: click through to their Company page → "About" → look for "Community," "Sustainability," or "Responsibility" links.
5. Search: `[company name] "youth" OR "women's sports" OR "education" site:linkedin.com` to check if their priorities match RSF.

**Companies to check manually in next round:**  
Intel, Levi Strauss, Kaiser Permanente, Wells Fargo (Bay Area HQ), Zoom, Palo Alto Networks, Nvidia, Workday

**Judgment calls:**  
This strategy was not run with AI tools this round due to LinkedIn access limitations. Lazaro will run this manually. The goal is to add 3–5 new corporate candidates to the long list for the next batch.

## How the Final 5 Were Selected

From the 20 candidates on the Long List, the Final 5 were selected using the following logic (directly from the SP3 brief):

1. **Leads with Strong ratings across the board** → Final 5 unless specific reason they don't fit RSF's stage.
2. **Leads with mixed ratings** → Pattern matters. Mission Alignment + Geography are the harder filters. A lead that fails both is excluded regardless of other strengths.
3. **Leads with Unknown on Partnership Size Fit** → Not automatically disqualified; 990 research was run to clarify before finalizing.
4. **Leads with any warm connection signal** → Even a Partial can elevate an otherwise middling lead.

| Adobe Foundation | Strong | Strong | Strong | Strong | ✅ Final 5 — Tier 1 |

## What to Do Differently Next Time

1. **Run LinkedIn CSR search earlier.** Strategy 3 was incomplete because AI tools can't search LinkedIn directly. Lazaro or Reggie should run this manually at the start of the next batch — it surfaces CSR roles at companies not found through other searches.

2. **Check 990s before scoring size fit.** Several candidates were rated "Unknown" or "Partial" on Partnership Size Fit. Running 990 lookups on ProPublica for each candidate before scoring would make the Long List more precise.

3. **Resolve Qwentin Brown's internship company.** This is a potentially warm lead that was left as "Unknown" this round. A quick conversation with Reggie or Zenith resolves it.

4. **Add Morgan State corporate sponsor mapping.** Morgan State's corporate partners haven't been mapped against Bay Area funders. This could surface 3–5 additional warm leads for the next batch.

5. **Track time-sensitive opportunities on a calendar.** Several leads have specific deadlines that were noted but not formalized:
   - NFL Foundation Player Matching: September 30, 2025
   - Joseph & Vera Long Foundation Fall Cycle: September 2025
   - Women's Sports Foundation (next cycle): 2027
   - Adobe Employee Community Fund: March 16–May 16 (annually)
   - USA Football Girls Flag Grant: January 2026

6. **Document which leads RSF actually contacted and the outcomes.** After this lead set is handed off, RSF should record: date of first outreach, response received, outcome. This data makes the next research round faster — you know which organizations are responsive, what framing worked, and what to avoid.

## Replicability Summary

Anyone running this process again needs three inputs:
1. **RSF's current confirmed relationships** (update the warm network map in Strategy 5)
2. **RSF's current programming and outcomes data** (update stats: youth served, placements, grants awarded)
3. **The current date** (check grant cycle deadlines; many programs are annual)

With those three inputs and the 5 search strategies above, a researcher can produce a new Long List of 15–20 candidates in 3–4 hours and shortlist to 5 leads in another 1–2 hours.
